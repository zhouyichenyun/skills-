# Narrative clarity gates

Use for narrative openings, suspense, rules stories and any repair after a viewer says “I cannot tell what is happening.” The director owns the plan; the reviewer judges the actual cut. Keep this pass short and separate from anatomy, continuity, loudness and export checks.

## Before spending: six answers on paper

1. **Viewpoint and objective:** whose immediate problem are we following, and what are they visibly trying to do? Names and biography can wait; the action cannot.
2. **Orientation:** what visible relation places the person, relevant object, threat and exit in the same story space? A wide shot is one option; a legible relation shot or continuous reveal can also do it. An isolated hand or device insert does not establish this by itself.
3. **Baseline and anomaly:** what ordinary condition makes the later contradiction perceptible? A number on a panel is not a contradiction until the audience can establish what it should count.
4. **Cause and response:** map `stimulus → person perceives it → action/choice → visible result`. Do not omit the perception or consequence that makes the next image intelligible.
5. **Sound relation:** for each crucial off-screen line/knock, identify the speaker/source, its location, who can hear it, and the shot or acoustic cue establishing that relation. Unknown identity can be a mystery; unknown basic relation cannot silently stand in for suspense.
6. **Information and duration:** each retained shot must add a fact, change a decision, redirect attention or sustain a specific expectation already established. Mark where its information finishes and why any remaining hold matters. Generated clip length and target runtime are not reasons to hold it.

Write two lines: `intentionally withheld mystery` and `facts viewers must understand now`. Suspense withholds an answer to an understood question. Confusion withholds the question itself. Do not require the viewer to read the bible, prompt, chronology notes or a later explanation to understand the present physical event.

If any answer is missing, PRE fails regardless of weighted score. Repair the beat/coverage plan before generation. Check the cheapest usable existing take first; supplement only the missing story information. Do not inflate the cut count to imitate a film.

## Coverage and editing handoff

For each causal beat, nominate actual coverage for orientation, action, perception/reaction and result. One well-staged shot may do several jobs; do not require a mechanical wide/close/reverse checklist. If a planned insert has no planted object or location, add the relation or redesign the beat. Distinguish separate camera coverage from crops of one unchanged plate.

Set edit points from visible action, a settled look, an established sound source, a decision, an information reveal or an earned hold. Keep enough frames to read the event and response; remove redundant resets and tails. Neither six equal-length takes nor rapid cutting is an artistic default. No effect or music cue can replace a missing causal link.

## Final sequence: first-view review

Use the actual final encoded video and its normal soundtrack. Watch once at normal speed without consulting the script or director notes, then write what was understood before replay. Record whether the reviewer already knew the story; do not call a context-informed check a blind audience test. An independent reviewer should not inherit the project explanation for the first-view pass.

Capture five short answers with the supporting time ranges:

- Who is trying to do what now?
- Where are the relevant people/objects in relation to each other?
- What happened, in cause-and-effect order?
- What is the specific abnormal fact or danger, and why does it matter?
- What clear unresolved question makes the next scene necessary?

Also mark any line whose speaker/addressee or referent is unclear, any sound whose relationship is unreadable, and any hold that outlasts its changing information/expectation. A viewer may not know a supernatural explanation; they must be able to follow the concrete event.

**Hard fail:** the event can only be explained by the script; a crucial sound/response has no readable relation; the reveal has no baseline; or omitted perception/choice/result prevents the causal chain. Q6/any other weighted score cannot compensate. **Pending:** full playback or sound review was unavailable, or only script/frames were examined. Neither state permits “story passed,” “cinematic quality passed,” or “final effects approved.”

## Repair routing

| Failure | Owner and smallest useful repair |
|---|---|
| Objective/causal order cannot be understood | Writer + director: simplify or reorder the beat, then specify what the audience will actually see/hear |
| Spatial, sound-source or reaction relation absent | Director: identify missing coverage; reuse a valid relation shot or design the minimum supplement |
| Good event is buried in waiting/repetition | Editor: trim actual takes, preserve the necessary reaction and recompute sound/subtitle timing |
| Correct plan was not rendered | Prompt/asset owner: repair only the failed shot after the plan is rechecked |

After repair, repeat first-view sequence review on the new rendered version. Do not patch a failed story with random transitions, universal speed-up, added exposition captions or more “cinematic” adjectives.
